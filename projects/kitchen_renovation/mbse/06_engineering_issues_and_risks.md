# Engineering Issues and Risks

## Issues

| ID | Issue | Status |
|---|---|---|
| ISSUE-001 | Required quantity of Starlon 3 mm underlay is not yet confirmed. The repair area is approximately 15.68 m². Purchasing strategy remains open: buy one package first and a second if required, or buy two initially. Final quantity depends on actual coverage, cuts and installation waste. | OPEN ISSUE |
| ISSUE-002 | A replacement laminate product has not yet been selected. The retained existing floor is identified by the user as Kaindl Classic Touch 37224, approximately 1382 × 193 × 7–8 mm, without a visible V-groove. Selection criteria include: (1) mandatory mechanical compatibility with the retained floor locking/joint profile, requiring a physical fit test; (2) adequate overall product quality; (3) adequate durability/wear resistance for the kitchen use case; and (4) adequate resistance to water/moisture exposure. Decorative match is not required. Exact minimum quality level, wear/use class, water-resistance performance and corresponding acceptance evidence are currently TBD and must be defined before final product selection. | OPEN ISSUE |
| ISSUE-003 | Floor-cavity depths of approximately 100 mm and 120 mm have been reported, while DEC-001 selects STEICO flex 036 in 100 mm thickness. Treatment of the approximately 120 mm cavities remains TBD. | OPEN ISSUE |
| ISSUE-004 | Water-management strategy above the upper OSB remains unresolved. The original build-up had no dedicated waterproof layer above the upper OSB. After an earlier repair by the previous owner, openings/joints between OSB boards at several locations were covered only with airtight construction tape. The current reconstruction must decide whether to keep an airtightness-only treatment at OSB joints or add a dedicated water-resistant/waterproof layer above the OSB before the underlay and laminate. Required water protection level, material/system, continuity at edges and service penetrations, compatibility with the timber-floor moisture design, and inspection/repair implications remain TBD. | OPEN ISSUE |
| ISSUE-005 | The reconstructed timber floor must accommodate the existing group of service penetrations rising from the floor near the wall, including plumbing/drainage and electrical/conduit routes visible in the current exposed installation area. The final detail is TBD and must address routing, mechanical protection, separation and future inspection/access while also preserving continuity of the floor thermal-insulation layer and restoring the required airtight layer around all penetrations. A specific sub-problem is how to seal pipes and other services where they pass through the OSB layer. Standard system solutions/products used for airtight service penetrations in timber construction should be identified and evaluated first; if no suitable standard solution is available for the actual geometry, a project-specific sealing detail should be developed. Exact airtightness plane, sealing products/details and treatment of insulation around each service remain TBD. | OPEN ISSUE |
| ISSUE-006 | Decide whether to reuse the dried original upper OSB boards or replace them with new OSB. The original boards are reported to be swollen. Candidate new boards are approximately 2 mm thinner, so partial replacement would create a local level step that may require compensation. The user has observed comparable OSB level steps in an earlier repair by the previous owner that were not noticeable in service once covered by the impact-sound underlay and laminate. The decision must weigh board condition/flatness, structural suitability, resulting floor level transitions, work required to compensate thickness differences, and uncertainty about possible hidden biological contamination in previously wetted original OSB. Presence of mould is not confirmed. | OPEN ISSUE |
| ISSUE-007 | Decide whether to install permanent moisture sensors inside the reconstructed floor assembly to provide early warning of future water ingress or abnormal moisture. A system such as Senzomatic is a candidate/example only and has not been selected. Evaluation should cover sensor placement, what material/layer is monitored, detection capability and alarm method, power/communication requirements, serviceability and replacement access, installation impact on the floor build-up, cost, and whether the expected benefit justifies installation. Final decision and product/system selection are TBD. | OPEN ISSUE |

| ID | Risk | Status |
|---|---|---|
